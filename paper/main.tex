\begin{abstract}%   <- trailing '%' for backward compatibility of .sty file
We introduce \emph{Bayesian-leaf Distributional Forests} (BDF), an evidence-regularized probabilistic tree-ensemble framework that replaces point-valued leaves with explicit predictive distributions.
BDF performs exact conjugate updates conditional on the stated empirical-Bayes quantities (e.g., Beta--Bernoulli, and the Normal--Normal mean update conditional on plug-in variance), and uses evidence-surrogate or regularized plug-in log scores for non-conjugate leaves such as Student-$t$ and KDE leaves.
Tree growth is driven by an additive leaf log-score objective together with a depth- and split-identity penalty, yielding scalable greedy forests whose predictions are mixtures of leaf-level predictive distributions.

The method is designed for the practical regime where full Bayesian posterior sampling over tree structures is too costly, but calibrated predictive distributions are still needed.
The resulting procedure is exact Bayes only at conjugate leaves and evidence-regularized elsewhere; we make this distinction explicit in the training objective, theory, and experiments.
Across real regression benchmarks, validation selects Student-$t$ leaves on 9/15 datasets; exact NLE is selected on 1/15, NLL+BIC on 7/15, and pure plug-in NLL on 7/15.
The fixed Normal--Normal NLE reference is close but not dominant (median $+3.03\%$ CRPS gap to BDF-Full, mean $+9.54\%$, better on 1/15 datasets).
QRF and DRF achieve the best aggregate CRPS/CRPSS, while BDF-Full is the strongest parametric probabilistic model by relative CRPS and attains the best average 90\% interval-score rank with nominal-to-slightly-conservative coverage.
In binary classification, BDF-Full is competitive with random forests, LightGBM, NGBoost, and calibrated variants, with the best average log-loss rank and no significant disadvantage against the strongest tree and boosting baselines under Holm-corrected paired tests; the fixed-NLE Beta--Bernoulli Core variant is within a median $+0.47\%$ log-loss of BDF-Full.
Overall, BDF provides a scalable route to likelihood-aware tree ensembles: it preserves the computational style of random forests while exposing probabilistic leaf models, Bayesian leaf uncertainty when available, and explicit regularization when exact evidence is unavailable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Tree ensembles---Random Forests \citep{breiman2001random} and Gradient Boosted Trees \citep{friedman2001greedy,chen2016xgboost,ke2017lightgbm}---are a dominant paradigm for tabular prediction due to their accuracy, robustness, and favorable computational profile.
Their strength comes from learning data-adaptive partitions of the covariate space while retaining interpretability through leaf-level structure and post-hoc tools (e.g., feature importance, partial dependence).
However, most deployed tree implementations are optimized for \emph{point targets} (conditional means/medians or class labels) and provide uncertainty only indirectly (e.g., ensemble variance, quantile losses, post-hoc calibration).
In many applications---risk-sensitive decision making, selective prediction, monitoring, and downstream optimization---one instead requires a coherent estimate of the \emph{full conditional distribution} $p(y\mid \mathbf{x})$.

A growing literature equips trees with probabilistic outputs.
One direction fits \emph{distributional} models in terminal nodes.
Distributional regression forests \citep{schlosser2019distributional} combine recursive partitioning with parametric distributional models (in the spirit of GAMLSS), selecting splits using likelihood/score-based criteria and fitting leaf parameters by maximum likelihood.
A second direction uses \emph{probabilistic boosting}, where the parameters of a chosen output distribution are learned via gradient boosting; examples include NGBoost \citep{duan2020ngboost} and distributional boosting frameworks such as XGBoostLSS \citep{marz2019xgboostlss}.
These approaches provide expressive conditional distributions, but they typically remain \emph{frequentist at the leaf level}: uncertainty is represented through fitted distributional parameters, while parameter uncertainty is handled implicitly (or ignored), and split decisions rely on plug-in likelihood objectives or boosting dynamics.

At the other end of the spectrum, fully Bayesian tree models place priors over tree structures and leaf parameters and perform posterior inference \citep{denison1998bayesian,chipman2010bart}.
While conceptually appealing, such methods often rely on MCMC over tree structures, which can be substantially more expensive than modern split search and less straightforward to deploy at scale or with diverse likelihood families.
Recent work revisits Bayesian tree induction from a MAP perspective (e.g., MAPTree) \citep{sullivan2024maptree}, but a core tension remains: \emph{Bayesian structure inference} is expensive, whereas \emph{fast split optimization} is central to how trees are used in practice.

This paper targets the regime in which tree ensembles are most effective: scalable split search, many candidate partitions, and the need for uncertainty-aware prediction without full posterior sampling over tree structures.
We introduce \emph{Bayesian-leaf Distributional Forests} (BDF), which upgrade classical forests to probabilistic predictors by combining \emph{Bayesian inference within leaves when available} with evidence-regularized fitted leaves otherwise, and by selecting splits via \emph{log predictive scores}.

\paragraph{Key idea: Bayesian leaf principles + log-score-based split selection.}
BDF treats each leaf as a local probabilistic model. For conjugate leaves this is a Bayesian model with prior $\pi(\theta_\ell\mid\eta)$, posterior $\pi(\theta_\ell\mid\mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta)$, and posterior predictive $p_\ell(y\mid \mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta)$, conditional on the empirical-Bayes quantities stated in Section~\ref{subsec:leaf_models}; for non-conjugate leaves it is an explicit fitted density scored by an evidence surrogate or by a regularized plug-in log score.
Tree induction is expressed through a summed leaf score $\sum_{\ell} S(\mathcal{D}_\ell)$, where $S$ is a negative log-score associated with the leaf predictor.
For conjugate likelihood--prior pairs, $S$ is the \emph{negative log evidence}, which admits a prequential interpretation as Bayesian cumulative log-loss.
When closed-form evidence is unavailable, the same objective is instantiated with a Laplace/BIC surrogate or a regularized plug-in log score, and the selected regime is reported empirically (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_real}, Table~\ref{tab:bdf-selection}).
A global MDL-style structural code controls tree growth through the depth-dependent logistic prior and tempered split-identity penalty in Section~\ref{subsec:complexity_growth}.

\paragraph{Scalable probabilistic ensembles.}
BDF is not a posterior sampler over tree structures.
Instead, it combines Bayesian or evidence-regularized probabilistic leaves with standard randomized ensemble training (bagging and feature subsampling), producing predictions by mixing leaf-level predictive distributions across trees.
This retains the computational profile of classical forests while yielding coherent predictive distributions.

\paragraph{Two empirical variants.}
We report two reviewer-facing variants.
\emph{BDF-Core} denotes a small canonical evidence-comparison set of fixed-NLE conjugate leaves: Normal--Normal NLE for regression and Beta--Bernoulli NLE for classification.
These are not the only conjugate pairs supported by the framework; they are the default real-valued and binary references used to isolate exact evidence scoring.
\emph{BDF-Full} denotes the practical validation-selected aggregate over the canonical leaf families and score regimes under one tuning budget per family.
For conjugate Normal--Normal/Beta--Bernoulli leaves, the selectable score regimes are NLE, NLL+BIC, and pure NLL; for non-conjugate Student-$t$ leaves, exact evidence is unavailable, so the selectable regimes are NLL+BIC and pure NLL.
Unless otherwise stated, headline benchmark numbers refer to BDF-Full; Appendix~\ref{app:bdf-leaf-regimes} enumerates the submitted leaf families, score regimes, and fitting status.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We introduce BDF, a scalable probabilistic tree ensemble whose leaves expose explicit predictive distributions: exact posterior predictives for conjugate leaves and regularized fitted densities otherwise.
\item We formalize greedy split selection through an additive leaf-wise log-score objective covering exact evidence, Laplace/BIC surrogates, and pure plug-in likelihood scores, combined with validation-tuned MDL-style structural penalties.
\item We connect conjugate evidence splitting to prequential log scoring, marginal-likelihood Occam penalties, and local Bayes-factor split tests, while making the fixed-partition scope of this theory explicit.
\item We empirically decompose BDF-Core and BDF-Full across leaf families and score regimes, showing where exact NLE is sufficient and where robust Student-$t$ or plug-in regimes are selected.
\item We show that BDF-Full is a competitive calibrated distributional predictor: QRF/DRF lead aggregate CRPS/CRPSS, while BDF-Full is the strongest parametric probabilistic model by relative CRPS and has the best average 90\% interval-score rank.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related-work}

BDF sits between scalable tree ensembles, distributional prediction, and Bayesian tree modeling. Random forests \citep{breiman2001random} and gradient-boosted trees \citep{friedman2001greedy,chen2016xgboost,ke2017lightgbm} are strong tabular predictors, but their native objectives typically target point functionals and leave uncertainty to post-hoc estimates, quantile losses, or calibration layers.

\paragraph{Distributional tree methods.}
Distributional regression forests \citep{schlosser2019distributional} fit distributional models in leaves and use likelihood/score-based splits; Distributional Random Forests \citep{cevid2022distributional} estimate conditional distributions through forest-induced weights. BDF differs by making the leaf likelihood explicit: conjugate leaves integrate leaf-parameter uncertainty, while broader fitted leaves use evidence surrogates or regularized plug-in scores. Mondrian forests \citep{lakshminarayanan2014mondrian} also provide scalable uncertainty-aware partitions, but BDF uses evidence-based greedy split selection and explicit leaf predictives.

\paragraph{Quantile and conformal prediction.}
Quantile regression forests \citep[QRF;][]{meinshausen2006quantile} provide a strong nonparametric baseline for intervals and CRPS. Conformal prediction \citep{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution,romano2019conformalized} gives distribution-free marginal coverage by calibrating held-out residual or quantile scores. These methods are complementary: QRF avoids parametric likelihoods, and conformal wrappers guarantee coverage without directly modeling the full conditional density.

\paragraph{Probabilistic boosting.}
NGBoost \citep{duan2020ngboost} and XGBoostLSS-style distributional boosting \citep{marz2019xgboostlss} learn distribution parameters with boosting dynamics and plug-in predictive likelihoods; Treeffuser \citep{beltranvelez2024treeffuser} uses tree-parameterized score models for conditional diffusion prediction. BDF instead keeps the probabilistic model local: distributional shape comes from explicit leaf likelihoods, Bayesian leaf updates where available, and mixtures over randomized partitions.

\paragraph{Bayesian tree models.}
Bayesian CART and BART \citep{denison1998bayesian,chipman2010bart} place priors over tree structures and leaf parameters and typically use MCMC over tree topologies. MAP-based Bayesian tree induction, such as MAPTree \citep{sullivan2024maptree}, improves scalability for single-tree structure inference. BDF is complementary: it does not sample or optimize a global posterior over forest structures, but adopts Bayesian inference where it is cheap and reusable---inside leaves---while retaining fast greedy split search and randomized ensembling.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}

Bayesian-leaf Distributional Forests (BDF) extend classical tree ensembles by replacing point-valued leaf predictions with \emph{probabilistic leaf distributions} and selecting partitions using \emph{log predictive scores}. For conjugate leaf families (e.g., Normal--Normal, Beta--Bernoulli, Gamma--Poisson), BDF uses closed-form posteriors, posterior predictives, and negative log evidence (NLE) split scores. For non-conjugate leaves, including the Student-$t$ leaves selected on most regression datasets (Table~\ref{tab:bdf-selection}), BDF uses fitted predictive distributions scored by a BIC-style evidence surrogate or a regularized plug-in log score. Thus BDF is exact Bayes only at conjugate leaves; elsewhere it is evidence-regularized rather than a posterior sampler over tree structures.

\subsection{Problem Setup and Partitions}

Let $\mathcal{D}_n = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$, with $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathcal{X}\subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ and $y_i \in \mathcal{Y}$.
A decision tree $T$ defines a partition of $\mathcal{X}$ into leaf regions $\{R_\ell\}_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}(T)}$, where $\bigcup_{\ell} R_\ell = \mathcal X$ and $R_i \cap R_j = \emptyset$ for all distinct $i, j \in \mathcal L(T)$, with $\mathcal{L}(T)$ denoting the set of leaves.
Each leaf $\ell$ corresponds to the local dataset
\[
\mathcal{D}_\ell = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\in \mathcal{D}_n:\ \mathbf{x}_i\in R_\ell\},
\qquad n_\ell = |\mathcal{D}_\ell|.
\]
For a query point $\mathbf{x}^\star$, let $\ell_T(\mathbf{x}^\star)$ denote its leaf assignment in $T$.

\subsection{Leaf Models and Bayesian Updates}\label{subsec:leaf_models}

Within each leaf $\ell$, BDF specifies a conditional model for $Y$ that is constant within the region:
\[
y \mid (\mathbf{x}\in R_\ell, \theta_\ell) \sim p(y \mid \theta_\ell),
\qquad
\theta_\ell \sim \pi(\theta_\ell \mid \eta),
\]
where $\theta_\ell$ are leaf parameters and $\eta$ are global hyperparameters shared across leaves.
The leaf posterior is
\[
\pi(\theta_\ell \mid \mathcal{D}_\ell, \eta)
\ \propto\
\pi(\theta_\ell \mid \eta)\prod_{(\mathbf{x}_i,y_i)\in\mathcal{D}_\ell} p(y_i\mid\theta_\ell).
\]
The leaf posterior predictive distribution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaf_ppd}
p_\ell(y^\star \mid \mathcal{D}_\ell, \eta)
=
\int p(y^\star \mid \theta_\ell)\,\pi(\theta_\ell \mid \mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta)\,d\theta_\ell.
\end{equation}
Conditional on the leaf parameter $\theta_\ell$ and the leaf assignment, observations within each leaf are i.i.d.; this conditional independence---rather than a separate exchangeability assumption---is what licenses the prequential factorization of the marginal likelihood (Section~\ref{subsec:split_scoring}). We note that this i.i.d.\ property holds \emph{conditional on a fixed partition}; when the partition is learned from data, the observations reaching a given leaf are selected by a data-dependent rule, and the i.i.d.\ assumption is only approximate. We return to this point in Section~\ref{subsec:theory_adaptive_caveat}.

\paragraph{Conjugate leaves.}
For conjugate likelihood--prior pairs (e.g.\ Normal--Normal, Beta--Bernoulli), the posterior, posterior predictive, and marginal likelihood are available in closed form. All updates reduce to sufficient statistics, enabling constant-time leaf re-scoring during split search. Concrete update equations for the leaf families used in this paper are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:conjugate_updates}.

\paragraph{Non-conjugate leaves.}
When the likelihood--prior pair is not conjugate, BDF requires only a tractable predictive density and an additive split score. The implementation exposes three score variants:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Laplace/BIC evidence surrogate}---plug-in negative log-likelihood plus $\tfrac{k_\ell}{2}\log n_\ell$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:bic_leaf}, justified as an $O(1)$ approximation to the NLE under standard regularity conditions when the plug-in estimate is an MLE/MAP (Section~\ref{subsec:theory_surrogates});
\item \emph{LOO- or $K$-fold-corrected predictive score}---used for KDE leaves where the BIC penalty is ill-defined ($k=1$ is not informative) but the leaf admits cheap leave-one-out updates;
\item \emph{Pure plug-in negative log-likelihood}---a pragmatic log-score variant regularized by the structural split penalty in Eq.~\eqref{eq:net_gain}, minimum leaf size, ensembling, and validation selection.
\end{enumerate}
Predictions from a non-conjugate leaf use its fitted leaf distribution rather than a posterior predictive. The Bayesian interpretation is exact for conjugate leaves, asymptotic for regular Laplace/BIC leaves, and evidence-regularized for the remaining plug-in variants. Concrete leaf specifications are in Appendices~\ref{app:conjugate_updates} and~\ref{app:nonconjugate_updates}.

\paragraph{Empirical Bayes hyperparameters.}\label{par:empirical_bayes}
By default, $\eta$ is set from training data only and tuned jointly with the other BDF hyperparameters on fold 0; Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark-protocol} gives the full leakage-free protocol. Normal--Normal leaves use a training-fold prior location and tune the shrinkage scale; the leaf variance is a leaf-local plug-in estimate, so the score is a profile/empirical-Bayes evidence conditional on that variance. Beta--Bernoulli leaves center the prior at the empirical class frequency and tune prior strength. In the theory below, $\eta$ is treated as fixed, so the exact marginal-likelihood factorization holds conditional on this empirical-Bayes choice.

\subsection{Split Scoring}\label{subsec:split_scoring}

BDF selects splits by minimizing an additive tree-level negative log score,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:additive_tree_score}
\mathrm{Loss}(T;\mathcal{D}_n)
=
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}(T)} S(\mathcal{D}_\ell),
\end{equation}
where $S(\mathcal{D}_\ell)$ is a leaf-level negative log score measuring the quality of the probabilistic predictor in leaf~$\ell$. For conjugate leaves, $S$ is the negative log marginal likelihood (evidence). For non-conjugate plug-in leaves, $S$ is the negative log-likelihood with an evidence surrogate such as BIC.

\paragraph{Negative log evidence (NLE) for conjugate leaves.}
The primary split criterion uses the marginal likelihood,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nle_leaf}
S(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
-\log m(\mathcal{D}_\ell),
\qquad
m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
\int \pi(\theta_\ell\mid\eta)\prod_{i:\mathbf{x}_i\in R_\ell}p(y_i\mid\theta_\ell)\,d\theta_\ell.
\end{equation}
Conditional on the leaf assignment and on $\eta$, the observations in leaf $\ell$ are i.i.d.\ given $\theta_\ell$, so the marginal likelihood admits a prequential (sequential predictive) factorization:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prequential}
m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
= \prod_{i=1}^{n_\ell} p(y_{\sigma(i)} \mid y_{\sigma(1)},\dots,y_{\sigma(i-1)},\eta),
\end{equation}
for any permutation $\sigma$ of the leaf indices, where each factor is the one-step-ahead posterior predictive. The NLE thus equals the cumulative log-loss of a sequentially updated Bayesian forecaster---a connection to prequential model validation that we formalize in Section~\ref{subsec:theory_prequential}.

\paragraph{BIC surrogate for non-conjugate leaves.}
When the marginal likelihood is not available in closed form, we use the Laplace/BIC surrogate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bic_leaf}
S_{\mathrm{BIC}}(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
-\log p(\mathcal{D}_\ell \mid \hat{\theta}_\ell^{\,\mathrm{MAP}})
+
\frac{k_\ell}{2}\log n_\ell,
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\theta}_\ell^{\,\mathrm{MAP}}$ is the posterior mode when a prior is specified, the MLE for purely plug-in leaves, and $k_\ell = \dim(\theta_\ell)$. The MAP and MLE coincide asymptotically under regularity conditions, and the $O(1)$ difference between them is absorbed into the remainder of the Laplace expansion (Section~\ref{subsec:theory_surrogates}). Under standard regularity conditions, $S_{\mathrm{BIC}}$ approximates the NLE up to $O(1)$ error as $n_\ell \to \infty$ \citep{schwarz1978estimating}. When a non-conjugate leaf uses faster method-of-moments parameters instead of an MLE/MAP, as in the default Student-$t$ benchmark configuration, the same term should be read as a BIC-sized complexity correction rather than as a formal Laplace evidence approximation. A theoretical comparison of the two criteria, including finite-sample behavior and implications for split selection under misspecification, is given in Section~\ref{subsec:theory_surrogates}.

\paragraph{Predictive-score variants and pure plug-in NLL.}
The additive framework also permits held-out or approximate out-of-sample log predictive scores, or the pure plug-in NLL without a Laplace/BIC term. In the experiments, the split-score variant is tuned with the leaf family: regression selects exact NLE on 1 of 15 datasets, NLL+BIC on 7, and pure plug-in NLL on 7 (Table~\ref{tab:bdf-selection}); the conjugate Normal--Normal variant remains within a median $+0.81\%$ CRPS of the selected variant (Table~\ref{tab:bdf-conjugate-vs-selected-gap}).

\paragraph{Split gain.}
Given a candidate split $s$ at leaf $\ell$ producing children $(\ell_L,\ell_R)$, the \emph{split gain} under score $S$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split_gain}
\Delta S(s;\ell)
=
S(\mathcal{D}_\ell) - S(\mathcal{D}_{\ell_L}) - S(\mathcal{D}_{\ell_R}).
\end{equation}
Since $S$ is a negative log score (lower is better), a positive gain $\Delta S > 0$ indicates that the two-leaf model provides a better probabilistic fit than the unsplit leaf. Under independent priors on $\theta_{\ell_L}$ and $\theta_{\ell_R}$ (as assumed throughout), $\Delta S(s;\ell)$ equals the log Bayes factor in favor of splitting when $S$ is the NLE; this connection is formalized in Section~\ref{subsec:theory_split_bf}.

\paragraph{Relation to distributional forests.}
Likelihood-based distributional forests \citep{schlosser2019distributional} split by plug-in likelihood improvements, while DRF \citep{cevid2022distributional} uses an MMD split criterion and implicit weighted-neighborhood predictions. BDF instead exposes an explicit predictive distribution in each leaf and optimizes a log-score objective that integrates out leaf parameters where evidence is available, or uses a stated surrogate otherwise. This is a tradeoff: QRF/DRF can be stronger nonparametric scorers, while BDF provides likelihood-aware leaves for tails, counts, binary outcomes, and density evaluation.


\subsection{Complexity Control and Greedy Growth}\label{subsec:complexity_growth}

We control tree complexity through a penalized splitting criterion. At each candidate leaf $\ell$ at depth $d$, we compare a \emph{stop model} $M_{\mathrm{stop}}$ (retain the current leaf) against a \emph{split model} $M_{\mathrm{split}}$ (partition into two children via candidate split $s$). Under factorized priors, the log posterior odds decompose as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:posterior_odds}
\log \frac{P(M_{\mathrm{split}} \mid \mathcal{D})}{P(M_{\mathrm{stop}} \mid \mathcal{D})}
\;=\;
\underbrace{\Delta S(s;\ell)}_{\text{data support}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\log \frac{P(M_{\mathrm{split}}, s)}{P(M_{\mathrm{stop}})}}_{\text{prior log-odds}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Structural prior and tempered selection penalty.}
The prior probability of splitting at depth $d$ follows a logistic model, $p(\mathrm{split} \mid d) = \sigma(-\alpha - \delta d)$, with $\alpha, \delta > 0$.
Conditional on splitting, we place a prior over split parameters $(j,c)$ by first selecting a feature uniformly and then a cutpoint uniformly among that feature's candidates. That is, $P(j) = 1/K$ and $P(c \mid j) = 1/m_j$, yielding a prior contribution $-\log K - \log m_j$ to the log-odds.

To account for dependence among candidates (e.g., correlated features or adjacent cutpoints inducing similar partitions), we introduce a tempering parameter $\gamma \in [0,1]$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prior_logodds_fixed}
\log \frac{P(M_{\mathrm{split}}, s)}{P(M_{\mathrm{stop}})}
\;=\;
-\alpha - \delta d - \gamma(\log K + \log m_j).
\end{equation}
When $\gamma = 1$, this recovers the split-identity component of the Bayesian model selection penalty under the specified prior, up to the child-stopping terms discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:split_bayes_factor}. When $\gamma < 1$, the penalty is reduced to reflect an effective number of distinguishable candidates smaller than the nominal count; $\gamma=0$ removes the split-identity multiplicity correction while retaining the structural depth penalty.

Substituting~\eqref{eq:split_gain} and~\eqref{eq:prior_logodds_fixed} into~\eqref{eq:posterior_odds} yields the \emph{net gain}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:net_gain}
\Delta_{\mathrm{net}}(s;\ell)
\;=\;
\Delta S(s;\ell) - \gamma\log(K \cdot m_j) - \alpha - \delta d.
\end{equation}
A split is accepted if and only if $\Delta_{\mathrm{net}}(s;\ell) > 0$, i.e., the data support exceeds the combined structural and selection-complexity penalties.
The depth penalty $\alpha + \delta d$ with $\delta > 0$ discourages excessively deep trees by requiring progressively larger gains to justify further splits.

\begin{remark}[Interpretation via description length]\label{rem:mdl}
The net gain~\eqref{eq:net_gain} has an MDL interpretation: $\Delta S$ is the reduction in data coding cost under the prequential code~\eqref{eq:prequential}, while $\alpha+\delta d$ and $\gamma\log(K\cdot m_j)$ encode tree growth and split identity. The tempering parameter $\gamma$ controls the effective number of distinguishable split candidates.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Hyperparameter selection.}
The penalty parameters $(\alpha, \delta, \gamma)$ are selected on the tuning fold jointly with the remaining BDF hyperparameters; see Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark-protocol}. Defaults are $\alpha = 0$, $\delta = 0.001$, $\gamma = 0.1$ when no tuning fold is available.

\paragraph{Greedy construction.}
We implement the criterion~\eqref{eq:net_gain} within a best-first greedy strategy (Algorithm~\ref{alg:bdf_tree}), which maintains a priority queue of candidate splits ordered by $\Delta_{\mathrm{net}}$ and expands the highest-gain split at each step.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Greedy construction of a single Bayesian distributional tree}\label{alg:bdf_tree}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Dataset $\mathcal{D}$, hyperparameters $\eta$, penalty parameters $(\alpha, \delta, \gamma)$
\STATE Initialize tree $T$ as a single root leaf $\ell_0$; compute sufficient statistics, the leaf posterior or fitted parameters, and leaf score $S(\mathcal{D}_{\ell_0})$
\STATE Find the best candidate split $s^*$ at $\ell_0$ maximizing $\Delta_{\mathrm{net}}(s;\ell_0)$ via~\eqref{eq:net_gain}
\IF{$\Delta_{\mathrm{net}}(s^*;\ell_0) > 0$}
    \STATE Push $(\ell_0, s^*, \Delta_{\mathrm{net}})$ onto priority queue $\mathcal{Q}$
\ENDIF
\WHILE{$\mathcal{Q}$ is not empty}
    \STATE Pop $(\ell, s, \Delta)$ with largest $\Delta$ from $\mathcal{Q}$
    \STATE Apply split $s$ to $\ell$, creating children $\ell_L, \ell_R$
    \FOR{each child $c \in \{\ell_L, \ell_R\}$}
        \STATE Compute sufficient statistics, the leaf posterior or fitted parameters, and score $S(\mathcal{D}_c)$
        \STATE Find the best candidate split $s_c$ at depth $d_c$ maximizing $\Delta_{\mathrm{net}}(s_c; c)$
        \IF{$\Delta_{\mathrm{net}}(s_c; c) > 0$}
            \STATE Push $(c, s_c, \Delta_{\mathrm{net}})$ onto $\mathcal{Q}$
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
\ENDWHILE
\RETURN Tree $T$ with leaf predictive objects $\{p_\ell(\cdot \mid \mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta)\}_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}(T)}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Ensembles and Probabilistic Prediction}\label{subsec:ensemble}

A Bayesian-leaf Distributional Forest is an ensemble of $M$ randomized trees $\{T^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^M$ trained independently (and thus fully parallelizable) using standard diversity mechanisms: bootstrap or row subsampling of training data, and random feature subsampling at each split.

For a test input $\mathbf{x}^\star$, each tree contributes the predictive distribution from its assigned leaf,
\[
p^{(m)}(y^\star\mid \mathbf{x}^\star,\mathcal{D}_n)
=
p_{\ell_{T^{(m)}}(\mathbf{x}^\star)}(y^\star \mid \mathcal{D}_{\ell_{T^{(m)}}(\mathbf{x}^\star)},\eta).
\]
BDF aggregates these as a uniform mixture:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forest_mixture}
p_{\mathrm{BDF}}(y^\star\mid \mathbf{x}^\star,\mathcal{D}_n)
=
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M p^{(m)}(y^\star\mid \mathbf{x}^\star,\mathcal{D}_n).
\end{equation}
This mixture can represent complex predictive shapes (e.g., multimodality, skewness) even when individual leaves are simple parametric families.

Equation~\eqref{eq:forest_mixture} is a randomized ensemble of probabilistic leaf predictors, not full Bayesian model averaging over tree topologies. Conjugate leaves provide exact posterior predictives; plug-in or KDE leaves provide the fitted predictive distribution specified by their leaf family.

\section{Interpretation and Local Guarantees}\label{sec:theory}

This section records the main guarantees needed to interpret BDF. The results are deliberately local: they condition on a fixed tree partition and fixed hyperparameters $\eta$, and therefore justify the leaf predictors and split scores rather than proving consistency of the full adaptive forest. Detailed proofs and auxiliary asymptotics are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Evidence as a Prequential Log Score}\label{subsec:theory_prequential}

For a fixed tree $T$, the prior factorizes over leaves,
$\pi(\theta\mid \eta,T)=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}(T)}\pi(\theta_\ell\mid\eta)$, and the marginal likelihood factorizes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global_nle_sum}
-\log p(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{X},T,\eta)
=
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}(T)} S(\mathcal{D}_\ell),
\qquad
S(\mathcal{D}_\ell)=-\log m(\mathcal{D}_\ell).
\end{equation}
Within each leaf, $m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)$ also has the prequential representation
\[
m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
\prod_{j=1}^{n_\ell} p(y_{\ell,j}\mid y_{\ell,1:j-1}, \eta),
\]
for any ordering of the observations in the leaf. Thus the NLE used for split scoring is exactly the cumulative log-loss of a sequential Bayesian forecaster. Since log-loss is strictly proper,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proper_log_score}
\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)\sim P_0}\big[-\log q(y\mid \mathbf{x})\big]
\ge
\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)\sim P_0}\big[-\log f_0(y\mid \mathbf{x})\big],
\end{equation}
with equality only at the true conditional density, minimizing expected NLE targets conditional KL risk for a fixed partition.

\subsection{Evidence, Complexity, and Surrogates}
\label{subsec:theory_occam}
\label{subsec:theory_surrogates}
\label{subsec:theory_tempered}
\label{subsec:theory_split_bf}

The evidence includes an automatic Occam penalty. For the exact leaf posterior,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:evidence_decomposition}
-\log m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
\underbrace{-\mathbb{E}_{\pi(\theta_\ell\mid \mathcal{D}_\ell)}
\big[\log p(\mathcal{D}_\ell\mid \theta_\ell)\big]}_{\text{posterior expected negative log-lik.}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\mathrm{KL}\!\left(\pi(\theta_\ell\mid \mathcal{D}_\ell)\,\Vert\,\pi(\theta_\ell\mid\eta)\right)}_{\text{Occam factor}}.
\end{equation}
This identity is the Bayesian source of small-leaf regularization: a split must improve fit enough to justify moving two child posteriors away from the prior rather than one parent posterior. The explicit threshold in~\eqref{eq:net_gain} adds structural and search penalties; with $\gamma<1$ the split-identity penalty is tempered to reflect candidate dependence.

For non-conjugate leaves fitted by MLE/MAP, BDF replaces the evidence by the BIC/Laplace surrogate from~\eqref{eq:bic_leaf}. Under standard regularity conditions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplace_bic_theory}
-\log m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
-\log p(\mathcal{D}_\ell\mid \hat{\theta}_\ell)
+
\frac{k_\ell}{2}\log n_\ell
+
O(1),
\end{equation}
so the approximation error in a split gain is lower order than the $O(n_\ell)$ signal of a beneficial split. For method-of-moments non-conjugate leaves such as the default Student-$t$ benchmark configuration, the same term is a BIC-sized complexity correction rather than a formal marginal-likelihood approximation. If the pure plug-in NLL is used, Eq.~\eqref{eq:laplace_bic_theory} no longer applies and complexity control comes from the structural penalty, minimum leaf size, and ensembling. Under independent child priors, the NLE gain is also the log Bayes factor for splitting:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bf_local}
\log \mathrm{BF}(s)
=
\log m(\mathcal{D}_{\Omega_L})+\log m(\mathcal{D}_{\Omega_R})-\log m(\mathcal{D}_\Omega)
=
\Delta S(s;\Omega),
\end{equation}
and BDF accepts a split precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bf_threshold}
\log \mathrm{BF}(s) >
\gamma\log(K \cdot m_j)+\alpha+\delta d .
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:bic_uniform} and Appendix~\ref{app:split_consistency} give the full Laplace and per-node split-selection arguments.

\subsection{Leaf Predictive Consistency}\label{subsec:theory_leaf_consistency}

For a fixed region $\Omega$, leaf inference with an explicit Bayesian posterior concentrates around the best-in-class density as the number of observations in that region grows.

\begin{proposition}[Leaf-level posterior concentration]\label{prop:leaf_concentration}
Let $\Omega \subset \mathcal{X}$ be fixed, and suppose that conditional on $\mathbf{X}\in\Omega$, responses are i.i.d.\ from density $f_0^\Omega$. Let $\{p_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}$ be a regular parametric leaf family with compact parameter space, smooth identifiable likelihood, positive definite local information at the well-specified or KL-optimal parameter $\theta^\star$, and prior density positive and continuous near $\theta^\star$. Then, as $n_\Omega\to\infty$, the posterior concentrates around $\theta^\star$. In the well-specified case it satisfies the usual Bernstein--von Mises approximation; under misspecification it concentrates around the KL projection. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppd_convergence}
\int |p_\ell(y \mid \mathcal{D}_\Omega, \eta) - p_{\theta^\star}(y)|\,dy \to 0 .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The proposition says that, conditional on the partition, each conjugate or explicitly Bayesian leaf predictive is asymptotically optimal within its chosen family. For non-conjugate plug-in leaves, the analogous target is the fitted family density selected by the regularized log-score procedure. The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_leaf_concentration}.

\subsection{Adaptive Partitions and Forest Mixtures}
\label{subsec:theory_adaptive_caveat}
\label{subsec:theory_forest}

The fixed-partition scope is important. In the actual algorithm, the data choose the partition and also fit either leaf posteriors or plug-in leaf parameters, so the theory above does not establish post-selection consistency of the greedily grown forest. BDF uses evidence penalties, validation-tuned structural penalties, row/feature subsampling, and ensembling to control this adaptivity in practice.

The forest mixture still has a simple log-loss property. For any test pair $(\mathbf{x},y)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture_jensen}
-\log p_{\mathrm{BDF}}(y \mid \mathbf{x})
= -\log\!\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_m p^{(m)}(y \mid \mathbf{x})\right)
\le \frac{1}{M}\sum_m \left[-\log p^{(m)}(y \mid \mathbf{x})\right],
\end{equation}
by Jensen's inequality. Thus mixture aggregation cannot be worse, in pointwise log-loss, than the average tree predictive.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

We introduced Bayesian-leaf Distributional Forests (BDF), an evidence-centered probabilistic tree ensemble that performs exact Bayesian inference within conjugate leaves and uses evidence surrogates or regularized plug-in scores elsewhere. This design keeps the training loop close to scalable tree induction while producing full predictive distributions. Empirically, BDF-Full occupies a useful accuracy--calibration tradeoff: QRF and DRF lead aggregate CRPS/CRPSS, but BDF-Full is the strongest parametric probabilistic regression model by relative CRPS, has the best 90\% interval-score rank, and retains relative RMSE closer to boosted-tree methods than to the nonparametric CRPS leaders.

The main limitations follow from this design. BDF depends on the chosen leaf family and prior strength, so misspecification can affect sharpness and calibration in low-data regions. Its theory justifies leaf predictors and split scores under fixed partitions, but does not prove consistency of the full adaptive forest. In practice, regression often selects non-conjugate Student-$t$ leaves and sometimes pure plug-in NLL, so those runs should be interpreted as evidence-regularized rather than strictly Bayesian. BDF is therefore a modular likelihood-aware forest, not a universal SOTA replacement for QRF/DRF across all distributional metrics.

Prediction-time cost is another practical limitation. BDF evaluates a mixture of per-tree predictive distributions rather than reading a scalar leaf value, and stabilizing a full predictive distribution can require more trees than stabilizing a mean. Caching leaf moments, distilling the mixture into a smaller parametric form, or evaluating fewer trees at inference are natural accelerations. Future work should also develop richer leaf families, hierarchical or adaptive priors, and stronger post-selection theory for greedy partitions.

Because BDF is a general-purpose uncertainty estimator for tabular prediction, its main societal risks arise through downstream use rather than through the algorithm in isolation. Predictive uncertainty can support risk-aware decisions, but it does not remove dataset bias, privacy concerns, or the need for domain validation. High-stakes deployments should therefore pair BDF with task-specific evaluation, calibration checks on the deployment population, and appropriate human oversight.

\section{Leaf Models and Evidence Scores}\label{app:leaf-models}\label{app:conjugate_updates}

This appendix collects the leaf models and score approximations used in the submitted experiments. For conjugate families we record the posterior, posterior predictive, log marginal likelihood, and sufficient statistics; for non-conjugate families we state the plug-in predictive distribution and the evidence surrogate or regularized log-score used during split search.

\subsection{Normal--Normal Mean Model with Leaf-Local Plug-in Variance}\label{app:conjugate_normal}

Let $\bar{y}_\ell = n_\ell^{-1}\sum_{i:\mathbf{x}_i\in R_\ell} y_i$ denote the leaf sample mean and let $\hat\sigma_\ell^2=s_\ell^2$ denote the Bessel-corrected sample variance in the leaf, floored at a small positive constant for numerical stability. Conditional on this plug-in variance, the mean update is the usual conjugate Normal--Normal update. The sufficient statistics are $(n_\ell, \sum_i y_i, \sum_i y_i^2)$ and are maintained in $O(1)$ per insertion or removal, so both $\bar y_\ell$ and $\hat\sigma_\ell^2$ are available during split search.

\paragraph{Posterior.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal_posterior}
\tau_\ell^{-2} \;=\; \tau_0^{-2} + \frac{n_\ell}{\hat\sigma_\ell^2},
\qquad
\hat{\mu}_\ell \;=\; \tau_\ell^{2}\!\left(\frac{\mu_0}{\tau_0^2} + \frac{n_\ell\,\bar{y}_\ell}{\hat\sigma_\ell^2}\right),
\qquad
\mu \mid \mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(\hat{\mu}_\ell,\tau_\ell^2).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Posterior predictive.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal_predictive}
p_\ell(y^\star \mid \mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta) \;=\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(y^\star;\; \hat{\mu}_\ell,\; \hat\sigma_\ell^2 + \tau_\ell^2\right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Profile log marginal likelihood.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal_log_marginal}
\begin{aligned}
\log m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
\;=\;& -\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(2\pi\left(\frac{\hat\sigma_\ell^2}{n_\ell}+\tau_0^2\right)\right)
-\frac{(\bar y_\ell-\mu_0)^2}{2(\hat\sigma_\ell^2/n_\ell+\tau_0^2)} \\
&\;-\frac{n_\ell-1}{2}\left(1+\log(2\pi\hat\sigma_\ell^2)\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is the evidence of the Normal mean model conditional on the leaf-local plug-in variance estimate, with the residual term evaluated at $\hat\sigma_\ell^2$; it is therefore an empirical-Bayes/profile evidence, not an integration over unknown variance.

\paragraph{Domination envelope.}
Let $\Theta = [\mu_{\min}, \mu_{\max}]$ be the compact mean interval required by Proposition~\ref{prop:leaf_concentration}(i), and condition on any leaf variance estimate bounded below by the numerical floor. Then $\sup_{\mu\in\Theta} \mathcal{N}(y;\mu,\hat\sigma_\ell^2) \le (2\pi\hat\sigma_\ell^2)^{-1/2}$ is finite; the corresponding fixed-variance envelope is invoked in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_leaf_concentration}(d) for the predictive $L^1$ convergence argument.

\begin{remark}[Plug-in observation variance]
The implementation estimates $\hat\sigma_\ell^2$ separately inside each candidate leaf from the data routed to that leaf. Consequently, the Normal--Normal benchmark should be read as exact Bayesian inference for the mean conditional on a leaf-local empirical-Bayes variance estimate; the Normal--Inverse-Gamma leaf is the fully conjugate alternative when variance uncertainty is integrated explicitly.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Beta--Bernoulli}\label{app:conjugate_beta}

Let $s_\ell = \sum_{i:\mathbf{x}_i\in R_\ell} y_i$ denote the count of positive outcomes; the sufficient statistics are $(n_\ell, s_\ell)$ and are maintained in $O(1)$.

\paragraph{Posterior.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta_posterior}
p \mid \mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta \;\sim\; \mathrm{Beta}\!\left(\alpha_0 + s_\ell,\; \beta_0 + n_\ell - s_\ell\right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Posterior predictive.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta_predictive}
p_\ell(y^\star = 1 \mid \mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta) \;=\; \frac{\alpha_0 + s_\ell}{\alpha_0 + \beta_0 + n_\ell}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Log marginal likelihood.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta_log_marginal}
\log m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
\;=\; \log B\!\left(\alpha_0 + s_\ell,\; \beta_0 + n_\ell - s_\ell\right) - \log B(\alpha_0, \beta_0),
\end{equation}
where $B(a,b) = \Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)/\Gamma(a+b)$ is the Beta function.

\paragraph{Domination envelope.} Since $\mathcal{Y}=\{0,1\}$, $\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}p(y\mid\theta)\le 1$ and $g\equiv 1$ is trivially integrable on the two-point sample space.

\subsection{Gamma--Poisson Count Leaves}\label{app:conjugate_poisson}

For non-negative integer targets, BDF uses the conjugate Gamma--Poisson model. We write the Gamma prior in shape-rate form,
\[
\lambda \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha_0,\beta_0),
\qquad
y_i\mid \lambda \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda),
\]
where $\mathbb{E}[\lambda]=\alpha_0/\beta_0$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\lambda)=\alpha_0/\beta_0^2$. The implementation also exposes the equivalent mean-variance parameterization
\[
\alpha_0=\mu_\lambda^2/v_\lambda,
\qquad
\beta_0=\mu_\lambda/v_\lambda,
\]
with \texttt{auto} values set from training-fold moments and tuned prior scale.

\paragraph{Posterior.}
Let $s_\ell=\sum_{i:\mathbf{x}_i\in R_\ell} y_i$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson_posterior}
\lambda\mid\mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta
\sim
\mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha_\ell,\beta_\ell),
\qquad
\alpha_\ell=\alpha_0+s_\ell,
\quad
\beta_\ell=\beta_0+n_\ell .
\end{equation}
The sufficient statistics are $(n_\ell,s_\ell)$.

\paragraph{Posterior predictive.}
Integrating out $\lambda$ gives a Negative-Binomial predictive distribution,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson_predictive}
p_\ell(y^\star=k\mid\mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta)
=
\mathrm{NegBin}\!\left(k;\alpha_\ell,\frac{\beta_\ell}{1+\beta_\ell}\right),
\end{equation}
where the second argument is the success probability in the SciPy convention. The predictive mean and variance are
\[
\mathbb{E}[Y^\star\mid\mathcal{D}_\ell]=\frac{\alpha_\ell}{\beta_\ell},
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}(Y^\star\mid\mathcal{D}_\ell)=
\frac{\alpha_\ell(1+\beta_\ell)}{\beta_\ell^2}.
\]

\paragraph{Log marginal likelihood.}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson_log_marginal}
\log m(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
\log\Gamma(\alpha_\ell)-\log\Gamma(\alpha_0)
+\alpha_0\log\beta_0
-\alpha_\ell\log\beta_\ell
-\sum_i \log(y_i!).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Non-Conjugate Leaf Models}\label{app:nonconjugate_updates}

Tuning selects a non-conjugate leaf family on 9 of 15 regression datasets, all using the Student-$t$ leaf (Table~\ref{tab:bdf-selection}). These leaves do not admit a closed-form Bayesian evidence; BDF supports BIC-style complexity correction for plug-in likelihood scores, a leave-one-out predictive score for KDE, and a pure plug-in NLL variant whose missing complexity term is absorbed by the architecture's tempered MDL-style structural penalty (Eq.~\eqref{eq:net_gain}), the minimum-leaf-size constraint, and ensembling. The BIC correction is a Laplace evidence surrogate for regular MLE/MAP non-conjugate fits; for the default method-of-moments Student-$t$ benchmark configuration, it is a BIC-sized complexity penalty rather than a formal marginal-likelihood approximation. The score regime is exposed as a tunable categorical \{\texttt{nll\_bic}, \texttt{nll}\} for Student-$t$ leaves and analogous LOO/pure-NLL choices for KDE leaves, and is selected per dataset by fold-0 CRPS along with the other hyperparameters; for Student-$t$ leaves the locked artifacts select pure NLL on 4 of 9 datasets and NLL+BIC on 5 of 9. Predictions from non-conjugate leaves use the fitted leaf distribution rather than a posterior predictive.

\subsubsection{Student-\texorpdfstring{$t$}{t} Leaves}\label{app:student_t_leaves}

Student-$t$ leaves are used for robust real-valued regression and are the most frequently selected leaf family in the regression benchmarks.

\paragraph{Frequentist Student-$t$.}
The plug-in model is
\[
y_i \mid \mu,\sigma,\nu \sim t_\nu(\mu,\sigma),
\qquad
\theta=(\mu,\sigma,\nu),
\]
with $\nu>2$ when the variance is used. The implementation estimates $\mu$ and $\sigma$ by method of moments, EM/IRLS, or numerical MLE, and estimates $\nu$ when it is not fixed. The default benchmark configuration uses method-of-moments estimators
\begin{equation}\label{eq:student_t_mom}
\hat\mu_\ell = \bar y_\ell,
\qquad
\hat\sigma_\ell^{\,2}
= s_\ell^2 \cdot \frac{\hat\nu_\ell - 2}{\hat\nu_\ell},
\qquad
\hat\nu_\ell =
\begin{cases}
\nu_{\max}, & \hat\kappa_\ell \le 3,\\[2pt]
\mathrm{clip}\!\left(4 + \dfrac{6}{\hat\kappa_\ell - 3},\,\nu_{\min},\,\nu_{\max}\right), & \hat\kappa_\ell > 3,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\bar y_\ell$ and $s_\ell^2$ are the leaf sample mean and (Bessel-corrected) sample variance, $\hat\kappa_\ell$ is the leaf sample (excess+3) kurtosis, and the implementation uses $(\nu_{\min},\nu_{\max})=(2.05,100)$. The positive-excess MoM formula is derived from the Student-$t$ excess-kurtosis identity $6/(\nu-4)$, so as empirical kurtosis grows the estimated $\nu$ approaches 4 from above. When $\hat\kappa_\ell \le 3$ (the leaf data are platykurtic), $\hat\nu_\ell$ saturates at the configured upper bound, and the leaf reduces to a near-Gaussian fit.

The split score is the plug-in negative log-likelihood with an optional BIC-style correction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:student_t_score}
S_{\mathrm{t}}(\mathcal{D}_\ell;c)
=
-\sum_{i:\mathbf{x}_i\in R_\ell}
\log t_{\hat\nu_\ell}(y_i;\hat\mu_\ell,\hat\sigma_\ell)
\;+\;
c\cdot\frac{k_\ell}{2}\log n_\ell,
\qquad
c\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
where $c=1$ applies the BIC-style correction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bic_leaf} and $c=0$ recovers the pure plug-in NLL discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:split_scoring}. The parameter dimension is $k_\ell=3$ when $\nu$ is estimated alongside $(\mu,\sigma)$ and $k_\ell=2$ when $\nu$ is fixed (e.g.\ to a configured default). For the method-of-moments estimator used in the locked Student-$t$ benchmark, this parameter count defines the size of the complexity correction; it should not be read as a full Laplace calculation around an MLE/MAP mode. The leaf predictive is the fitted density
\[
p_\ell(y^\star\mid\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
t_{\hat\nu_\ell}(y^\star;\hat\mu_\ell,\hat\sigma_\ell),
\]
which is not a posterior predictive in the conjugate sense. With MLE/MAP parameters, the $c=1$ score has the Laplace interpretation of Appendix~\ref{app:approximate-bayesian-calculations-non-conjugate}; with the benchmark MoM parameters it is a BIC-sized regularization term. The $c=0$ score is the evidence-regularized log-score variant of Section~\ref{subsec:split_scoring}. In the locked artifacts, $c=0$ is selected on 4 of the 9 datasets where the Student-$t$ leaf is the chosen family.

\subsubsection{KDE Leaves}\label{app:kde_leaves}

KDE leaves are non-parametric predictive leaves used when a unimodal parametric likelihood is too restrictive. They do not have a closed-form Bayesian evidence in the current implementation.
For a leaf with centers $\{y_i\}_{i=1}^{n_\ell}$ and bandwidth $h_\ell>0$, the predictive density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kde_predictive}
p_\ell(y^\star\mid\mathcal{D}_\ell,h_\ell)
=
\frac{1}{n_\ell h_\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{n_\ell}
K\!\left(\frac{y^\star-y_i}{h_\ell}\right),
\end{equation}
with Gaussian or Epanechnikov kernels. Bandwidth is either fixed, Scott/Silverman rule-of-thumb, or pseudo-Bayesian shrinkage toward a global bandwidth $h_0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kde_bandwidth_shrinkage}
\log h_\ell^{\mathrm{post}}
=
\frac{m_h\log h_0+n_\ell\log h_\ell^{\mathrm{local}}}{m_h+n_\ell}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Split score.}
The default KDE score is leave-one-out negative log predictive density,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kde_loo}
S_{\mathrm{KDE\text{-}LOO}}(\mathcal{D}_\ell)
=
-\sum_{i=1}^{n_\ell}
\log\left[
\frac{1}{(n_\ell-1)h_\ell}
\sum_{j\ne i}
K\!\left(\frac{y_i-y_j}{h_\ell}\right)
\right],
\end{equation}
which is the LOO/$K$-fold-corrected log-score variant of Section~\ref{subsec:split_scoring} specialized to a one-parameter kernel density estimator. The BIC penalty $\tfrac{k_\ell}{2}\log n_\ell$ with $k_\ell=1$ is not informative for KDE (one effective parameter, the bandwidth, that is selected from a discrete rule-of-thumb set), so it is excluded as an alternative; pure plug-in NLL is available as a fallback and is rarely selected in our experiments. For computational efficiency, the split search may use a parent bandwidth for all candidate children and refine the top candidate splits with child-specific bandwidths.

\paragraph{Mixture evaluation.}
For all sample-based leaves, including KDE and Student-$t$, the forest predictive in Eq.~\eqref{eq:forest_mixture} is evaluated by drawing from the assigned leaf distribution in each tree and pooling samples across trees. CRPS, PIT, central intervals, and interval scores are then computed from the pooled samples; quantile-native baselines such as QRF use their reported quantile grid directly.

\subsection{Laplace Approximation for Non-Conjugate Leaves}
\label{app:approximate-bayesian-calculations-non-conjugate}

When the likelihood--prior pair is not conjugate, a Laplace expansion around the MAP estimate gives the evidence surrogate used by the BIC split score. This section records that calculation; the locked Student-$t$ benchmark variants use method-of-moments fitted predictive densities at prediction time, as described in Appendix~\ref{app:student_t_leaves}, so their BIC term should be read as a BIC-sized complexity correction.

\paragraph{MAP estimate and curvature.}
Let $\ell_n(\theta_\ell) = \sum_{i:\mathbf{x}_i\in R_\ell} \log p(y_i\mid\theta_\ell)$ denote the leaf log-likelihood. The leaf posterior mode is
\[
\hat{\theta}_\ell^{\mathrm{MAP}} \;=\; \arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\, \big\{\log \pi(\theta\mid\eta) + \ell_n(\theta)\big\},
\]
obtained by a few iterations of Newton's method warm-started at the parent's MAP. The posterior curvature at the mode is
\[
H_\ell \;=\; -\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\!\left[\log\pi(\theta\mid\eta) + \ell_n(\theta)\right]\!\big|_{\theta=\hat{\theta}_\ell^{\mathrm{MAP}}}.
\]

\paragraph{Gaussian posterior approximation.}
Under the regularity conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:leaf_concentration}, the Laplace approximation gives
\[
\pi(\theta_\ell\mid\mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta) \;\approx\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(\theta_\ell;\; \hat{\theta}_\ell^{\mathrm{MAP}},\; H_\ell^{-1}\right),
\]
with approximation error $o(1)$ in total variation as $n_\ell\to\infty$.

\paragraph{Approximate posterior predictive interpretation.}
If one carries the Laplace approximation through to prediction, the induced approximate posterior predictive would be
\[
p_\ell(y^\star\mid \mathcal{D}_\ell,\eta) \;\approx\; \int p(y^\star\mid\theta)\,\mathcal{N}\!\left(\theta;\,\hat{\theta}_\ell^{\mathrm{MAP}},\, H_\ell^{-1}\right) d\theta,
\]
evaluated in closed form for Gaussian-linearized likelihoods and numerically (Gauss--Hermite quadrature in low dimensions) otherwise. The locked Student-$t$ benchmark variants do not use this posterior-predictive integral at prediction time; they use the fitted Student-$t$ density described in Appendix~\ref{app:student_t_leaves}. In this paper the Laplace calculation justifies BIC evidence surrogates for MLE/MAP split scoring and motivates the BIC-sized correction used by faster plug-in variants, but it does not imply exact Bayesian prediction for the selected non-conjugate variants.

The associated BIC approximation to the log marginal likelihood, together with its validity as a surrogate for the NLE in split selection, is established in Appendix~\ref{app:bic_uniform}.
